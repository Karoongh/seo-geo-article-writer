# E-E-A-T, Sources, and Claim Handling

E-E-A-T (experience, expertise, authoritativeness, trust) is not a ranking switch or a score. It is the quality bar Google's systems and human raters aim to approximate, and trust is the centre of it. In practice it means: a reader can tell who made this, why they should believe it, and where the claims come from.

## Google's Who / How / Why check (from "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content")
- **Who:** Is the author clear? Bylines where readers expect them, linking to real background. No invented names, stock-photo "experts", or false credentials.
- **How:** If automation or AI helped produce the content, readers should be able to tell where it matters. Consider a short disclosure on pages where readers would ask how it was made. Never imply human testing or experience that did not happen.
- **Why:** The page exists to help people who arrive, not to catch search traffic. If the honest answer to "why does this page exist" is "for the keyword", fix the plan, not the wording.

When drafting for a business, tell the user which of these they must supply: real author name and bio, reviewer (for medical/legal/financial), real photos, real experience notes. Offer placeholders marked `[TO CONFIRM]`; never fill them with plausible inventions.

## Source hierarchy
1. Official documentation, standards bodies, regulators, manufacturer manuals and service bulletins
2. Peer-reviewed research and highly reputable technical publications
3. Established expert sites with named authors and editorial standards
4. Practitioner forums, reviews, user reports: only as supporting signal, labeled as such
Do not cite a source you have not opened. Prefer primary over a blog summarizing it. Record title and URL for each source used; the final check lists them.

## Claim labels (use the category in the wording, not only in your head)
| Label | Wording example | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Official fact | «طبق دفترچه راهنمای سازنده…» | A named primary source |
| Practitioner experience | «تعمیرکاران معمولاً…» / «در تجربه‌ی ما…» | Only if the user supplied it; never invent |
| Probable cause | «محتمل‌ترین علت…» | Say what raises or lowers likelihood |
| Confirmed cause | «پس از آزمایش X مشخص می‌شود…» | A test that confirms it |
| User report | «کاربران در انجمن‌ها گزارش داده‌اند…» | Weak; say so |

Numbers (prices, times, thresholds, error codes, dosages) need a source or must be left out. Say "هزینه به عوامل زیر بستگی دارد" and list the factors rather than inventing a range.

## YMYL and risk topics
Health, money, legal, safety-critical vehicle or electrical work, and anything where a wrong answer can hurt someone:
- State the danger first when continuing is unsafe ("اگر ... ، ادامه‌ی رانندگی را متوقف کنید").
- Do not give step-by-step instructions whose failure mode is injury or fire; describe what a professional will check.
- Say what cannot be determined remotely.
- Recommend named professional help types, not vague "see an expert".
- Suggest a qualified reviewer's name and date on the page; do not invent one.
- Run the independent fact-check pass described in SKILL.md.

## Local trust signals (when the business is location-based)
Real address and map link, phone/WhatsApp/Telegram the user supplied, consistent name-address-phone across the site and directories, genuine reviews only, real photos. Never fabricate reviews, ratings, or "years of experience".
